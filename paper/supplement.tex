\section{Reproducibility scope}

This supplement reports only experiments derived from the public, version-pinned
ChEMBL 37 SQLite release. The VietHerb-dependent multiple-instance learning
(MIL) arms are implementation-tested but prospectively locked; no synthetic or
restricted row contributes to a scientific estimate. All five-seed experiments
used seeds 13, 37, 71, 101, and 137. Each run manifest records dataset, metric,
prediction, and configuration hashes. The machine-readable tables distributed
beside this PDF contain the unrounded values underlying the manuscript.

\section{Cohort construction}

The source archive was \texttt{chembl\_37\_sqlite.tar.gz}, DOI
10.6019/CHEMBL.database.37. Its SHA-256 begins
\texttt{33c203740555f960}; the extracted SQLite digest begins
\texttt{4be13df3b68e25dc}. Full digests are stored in the machine-readable
extraction and evidence manifests.
Target identity was asserted against single human proteins and UniProt entries
P22303 (AChE), P35354 (PTGS2), and P27487 (DPP4).

All retained relations were equality measurements. Exact standardized
structure--target replicates were summarized by median pChEMBL. A pair whose
replicates spanned at least one log unit was contradictory. The primary binary
cohort excluded 435 contradictory and 3,353 gray-zone pairs; activity was
pChEMBL $\geq 6$ and inactivity was pChEMBL $\leq 5$. The continuous cohort
excluded the same 435 contradictory pairs but retained the gray zone.

The primary and continuous partitions used connected components at Morgan
Tanimoto $\geq0.70$ and had zero standardized-structure, InChIKey, Murcko
scaffold, and component overlap. The temporal cohort used the first source year:
training through 2017, validation from 2018 through 2021, and test from 2022
through 2025. It excluded 60 rows without year information and censored 343
future observations from training or validation aggregation. Chemical overlap
was allowed by design in this chronological sensitivity.

\section{Models and selection}

All fingerprint models used 2,048-bit chiral radius-2 Morgan fingerprints.
Neural MLPs used hidden widths 1,024 and 512, dropout 0.25, AdamW at learning
rate $3\times10^{-4}$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size 256, and patience
12. Logistic regression was L2-regularized. Extra Trees and logistic models were
fit per target. Chemprop 2.2.1 consumed the same split column and masked missing
labels, with a maximum of 100 epochs and patience 12.

Every tunable candidate was ranked by the mean validation objective over the
prespecified seeds. The test partition was evaluated only after the selected
configuration or best validation checkpoint was restored. Chemprop used
validation loss for deterministic checkpoint selection because exact-seed CUDA
execution made its online PRC metric incompatible with deterministic algorithms;
the sealed-test PR-AUC and all secondary metrics were computed independently
from stored predictions. Chemprop class-balanced sampling was disabled because
the sampler produced zero batches for sparse multitask rows.

\section{Natural-product subgroup}

The ChEMBL molecule-dictionary natural-product field was joined after applying
the same structure standardization as the frozen cohort. The source database
identified COCONUT 2025-07 as the flagging resource. Of 14,458 mapped molecules,
657 were flagged; 402 overlapped the primary binary cohort and 79 occurred in
its test set.

\section{Locked VietHerb experiment}

The implemented plant-bag branch represents each species as a bag of reported
metabolites, applies gated attention, and optimizes observed plant uses with
non-negative positive--unlabelled risk. The required real-data comparison is
activity-only versus naive broadcast versus bag MIL versus shuffled-label and
random-label controls under chemical and botanical cold starts. It will not be
unlocked until (i) written terms permit a reproducible VietHerb access route and
(ii) two domain reviewers complete and adjudicate the therapeutic-use mapping.
Accordingly, this supplement contains no estimate for the central
ethnopharmacological effect.
