\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Background:}
Traditional-medicine databases record uses at plant level, whereas bioactivity
databases measure compound--target relations. We establish a leakage-controlled
activity benchmark and a prospectively locked multiple-instance learning (MIL)
protocol for testing plant-level supervision without broadcasting a use label
to every reported constituent.

\textbf{Methods:}
We curated ChEMBL 37 measurements for human DPP4, acetylcholinesterase, and
COX-2. A 0.70 Morgan-Tanimoto connected-component split defined the primary
chemical out-of-distribution (OOD) test; a first-publication-year split and a
continuous-pChEMBL endpoint were sensitivities. Logistic regression, Extra
Trees, separate and shared fingerprint multilayer perceptrons (MLPs), and an
official Chemprop D-MPNN were run with five fixed seeds. Selection and early
stopping used validation data only; ranking, enrichment, discrimination, and
calibration were computed on the sealed test set.

\textbf{Results:}
Among 43,843 candidates, 16,941 records yielded 10,698 labelled pairs and
10,686 molecules after curation. No identity, Murcko scaffold, or chemical
component crossed primary partitions; maximum test-to-train Tanimoto was
0.698. Extra Trees achieved the highest chemical-OOD macro PR-AUC
($0.9425\pm0.0009$), followed by logistic regression
($0.9334\pm0.0000$), separate MLPs ($0.9281\pm0.0035$), shared MLP
($0.9203\pm0.0024$), and D-MPNN ($0.8938\pm0.0063$). The shared-minus-separate
MLP difference was $-0.0077$ (cluster bootstrap 95\% CI $-0.0141$ to
$-0.0021$), driven by acetylcholinesterase after multiplicity correction.
Conversely, the shared MLP improved temporal macro PR-AUC
($0.9425\pm0.0024$ versus $0.9247\pm0.0029$), but worsened continuous-pChEMBL
MAE ($0.8181\pm0.0049$ versus $0.8083\pm0.0066$).

\textbf{Conclusions:}
Model ordering was endpoint-, split-, target-, and metric-dependent; simple
fingerprint ensembles remained strong controls. The benchmark validates the
activity branch and quantifies negative transfer, but does not test the
ethnopharmacological MIL hypothesis, which remains locked pending redistributable
VietHerb data and expert-audited therapeutic-use mappings.

\textbf{Scientific Contribution:}
We provide a version-pinned, leakage-audited, five-seed benchmark that evaluates
multi-target sharing under chemical-OOD, temporal, and continuous endpoints.
Unlike naive herb-to-compound label propagation, the accompanying protocol
formalizes plant knowledge as positive--unlabelled bag supervision and requires
falsification controls before any ethnopharmacological claim.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} chemical out-of-distribution evaluation; ChEMBL;
multi-task learning; negative transfer; multiple-instance learning; natural
products; virtual screening

\section{Introduction}

Natural products remain an important source of chemical diversity for drug
discovery, but their biological evidence is heterogeneous and frequently
recorded at a different level from the molecule being screened. VietHerb was
created to organize Vietnamese medicinal plants together with metabolites,
therapeutic effects, morphology, and geography. Its original release reported
2,881 species, 10,887 metabolites, and many-to-many species--metabolite and
species--effect relations \citep{nguyenvo2019vietherb}. This local resource is
scientifically valuable, but a reported use of a plant is neither evidence that
every constituent is active nor proof of clinical efficacy.

Prior computational ethnopharmacology already links traditional symptoms,
efficacies, herbs, ingredients, and molecular targets. HTINet and HTINet2 use
heterogeneous networks and knowledge graphs for herb--target prediction
\citep{wang2019htinet,duan2024htinet2}; other systems use clinical symptom
profiles or multimodal ingredient--target information
\citep{cheung2026novelhti,liang2025multiiti}. Consequently, the broad claim that
traditional knowledge has not been used for target prediction is untenable.
The unresolved issue addressed here is narrower: VietHerb annotations are
plant-level labels, while ChEMBL observations are compound-level measurements.
This is naturally a multiple-instance learning (MIL) problem, in which a plant
is a bag and its reported metabolites are instances
\citep{ilse2018attention,doran2012mil}.

Reliable evaluation is equally important. Ligand benchmarks can reward
memorization when close analogues cross partitions \citep{wallach2018memorization},
and conventional scaffold splitting does not eliminate every form of chemical
redundancy. Traditional-medicine data add botanical leakage because the same
metabolite can occur in several species. Missing compound--target and
plant--use edges also cannot automatically be interpreted as negatives, making
positive--unlabelled (PU) learning a relevant sensitivity analysis
\citep{kiryo2017nnpu,wang2026prodti}.

We make three empirical and one prospective contribution. First, we release an
auditable ChEMBL 37 cohort with connected-component chemical-OOD and temporal
partitions. Second, we compare linear, tree-ensemble, fingerprint-neural, and
message-passing baselines across ranking, early enrichment, discrimination, and
calibration. Third, we test whether the sign of multi-target transfer persists
under temporal classification and continuous potency prediction. Finally, we
specify cross-granularity plant supervision as attention MIL with
positive--unlabelled labels and explicit shuffled-label controls. This last
hypothesis is deliberately not evaluated without a legally redistributable
VietHerb snapshot and expert-audited mappings.

\section{Materials and methods}

\subsection{Data sources and governance}

VietHerb provides plant--metabolite and plant--therapeutic-effect relations
\citep{nguyenvo2019vietherb}. Because no explicit database redistribution
licence was found during our audit, the confirmatory analysis requires a
maintainer-authorized export. Raw rows will not be redistributed without
written permission. Published artifacts will otherwise contain code, schemas,
source identifiers, checksums, aggregate counts, and split-generation logic.
Traditional-use records are treated as hypothesis-generating cultural and
historical evidence, not as validated biomedical claims.

For the confirmatory study, measured activities were extracted from the
versioned ChEMBL 37 SQLite release; the archive, database, and output were
bound by SHA-256 hashes. The earlier live REST snapshot is retained only as an
exploratory engineering analysis. ChEMBL is a
manually curated, large-scale bioactivity resource with explicit assay and
document provenance \citep{zdrazil2024chembl}. LOTUS may be used only for an
optional occurrence/provenance sensitivity analysis
\citep{rutz2022lotus}. Aggregated natural-product databases are not assumed to
provide independent activity ground truth \citep{chandrasekhar2025coconut}.

\subsection{Target-activity cohort}

The pilot targets are human dipeptidyl peptidase 4 (DPP4, CHEMBL284),
acetylcholinesterase (AChE, CHEMBL220), and prostaglandin G/H synthase 2
(COX-2/PTGS2, CHEMBL230). The primary cohort contains high-confidence,
single-protein biochemical binding assays with standardized IC$_{50}$ or
$K_i$ values in nM. Functional assays form a separate sensitivity cohort.
Records with data-validity warnings, duplicate flags, unresolved structures, or
ambiguous target assignments are excluded. Exact standardized
structure--target replicates are aggregated by median pChEMBL; pairs spanning
at least one log unit are flagged as contradictory and excluded from the
primary analysis.

The prespecified binary endpoint labels pChEMBL $\geq 6$ as active and
pChEMBL $\leq 5$ as inactive; the interval $(5,6)$ is excluded as a gray zone.
Only exact (equality) measurements enter the primary binary cohort. Alternative
cutoffs and continuous pActivity models are sensitivity analyses. Unmeasured
compound--target pairs remain masked. All stereochemistry and structure
standardization decisions, rejected rows, release metadata, and SHA-256 hashes
are retained in a machine-readable manifest.

\subsection{Therapeutic-use normalization}

Original text is preserved. Candidate mappings are retrieved from frozen
MeSH/Mondo releases, after which a language model may rank candidates but may
not accept a label automatically. The stored record includes source span,
ontology version, candidate list, confidence, model/prompt hash, abstention,
and reviewer decisions. Two pharmacognosy or pharmacy reviewers independently
annotate a prespecified stratified sample before adjudication. We report exact
and ancestor-aware precision, recall, F1, raw agreement, and Cohen's $\kappa$;
sample size is selected for a target confidence-interval width rather than fixed
solely by convenience.

The first model uses three coarse, clinically interpretable families:
metabolic/diabetes, cognition/neurological use, and pain/inflammation. ATC is
not used as the primary disease ontology because it classifies active drug
substances rather than diseases or symptoms.

\subsection{Molecular and botanical split construction}

Molecules are standardized globally and assigned once across all targets and
plants. Two locked regimes answer different questions. The chemical-OOD regime
groups molecules by a prespecified fingerprint clustering rule and reports
train--test nearest-neighbour Tanimoto distributions. A scaffold component split
is retained as a reproducible secondary regime. The botanical cold-start regime
holds out plant taxa and explicitly handles metabolites shared between taxa.

For the strict joint split, molecule nodes are joined when they share a chemical
group or occur in the same plant. Entire transitive connected components are
assigned to train, validation, or test. Thus, if compound A is chemically linked
to B and B occurs with C in one species, all three remain in one partition. No
feature transformation, loss weight, class prior, or hyperparameter is fitted
using test data.

The primary activity benchmark joined standardized molecules when their
radius-2 Morgan fingerprint Tanimoto similarity was at least 0.70. Whole
connected components were allocated 70:15:15 with a fixed split seed. An audit
then required zero overlap in standardized structure, InChIKey, Murcko scaffold,
and connected-component identifier. This scaffold-disjoint property is stricter
than the construction rule and was checked rather than assumed.

The temporal sensitivity used each molecule--target pair's earliest source
document year. Training contained first reports through 2017, validation
contained 2018--2021, and test contained 2022--2025. Measurements published
after the relevant training or validation cutoff were censored before label
aggregation. Unlike the primary regime, temporal splitting intentionally did
not enforce scaffold disjointness: it asks how a model trained on historical
chemistry behaves on later publications, including later measurements of known
series.

\subsection{Multiple-instance multi-task model}

Let $x_{ij}$ denote metabolite $j$ reported in plant $i$, and let
$h_{ij}=f_\theta(x_{ij})$ be its shared molecular representation. The primary
heads predict measured compound--target activities with masked, class-weighted
binary cross entropy. For each plant bag, gated attention computes
\begin{align}
e_{ij} &= w^\top\left[\tanh(Vh_{ij}) \odot \sigma(Uh_{ij})\right],\\
a_{ij} &= \frac{\exp(e_{ij})}{\sum_k \exp(e_{ik})},\\
z_i &= \sum_j a_{ij}h_{ij},
\end{align}
followed by one plant-use head per therapeutic family
\citep{ilse2018attention}. Attention weights provide instance attribution but
are not interpreted as causal explanations.

An observed plant use is positive and absence is unlabelled. Auxiliary heads
therefore use non-negative PU risk \citep{kiryo2017nnpu}. For class prior
$\pi$, positive risk $R_p^+$, positive-as-negative risk $R_p^-$, and unlabelled
negative risk $R_u^-$, the unbiased estimate is
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{PU}}=\pi R_p^+ + R_u^- - \pi R_p^- ,
\end{equation}
with the non-negative correction applied when the empirical negative component
falls below zero. Priors are frozen from training-only estimation and varied in
sensitivity analyses. The total objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{activity}}+
\lambda\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{plant\text{-}MIL}}.
\end{equation}

The compute-feasible encoder is a radius-2, 2,048-bit chiral Morgan fingerprint
followed by a two-layer MLP. This deliberately separates the prospective MIL
test from an expensive foundation model. The measured-activity benchmark also
included an official Chemprop directed message-passing neural network (D-MPNN)
\citep{yang2019chemprop} using identical split IDs.

\subsection{Baselines and falsification controls}

Measured-activity baselines were per-target L2-regularized logistic regression,
per-target Extra Trees, separate target MLPs, a hard-sharing multi-target MLP,
and a shared Chemprop 2.2.1 D-MPNN. Logistic regression and Extra Trees used the
same Morgan features and split assignments as the MLPs. The D-MPNN consumed
SMILES and three masked activity columns; missing compound--target observations
did not contribute to its loss.

The future VietHerb matrix additionally contains naive plant-label broadcasting,
bag-level MIL, shuffled-use-label MIL, prevalence-matched random auxiliary
labels, and optional domain importance weighting. Controls shuffle labels only
among training plants and equalize parameter and update budgets. The primary
future comparison is MIL versus the architecture-matched activity-only model;
naive broadcasting is an ablation, not the proposed method.

\subsection{Training and statistical analysis}

The MLP used hidden dimensions 1,024 and 512, dropout 0.25, AdamW with learning
rate $3\times10^{-4}$ and weight decay $10^{-4}$, batch size 256, and early
stopping patience 12 on validation macro PR-AUC. The D-MPNN used the official
Chemprop 2.2.1 defaults, at most 100 epochs, patience 12, and validation loss
for deterministic checkpoint selection. Validation PR-AUC was not used for the
D-MPNN because exact-seed CUDA execution invokes a non-deterministic cumulative
sum in that metric; all test metrics were instead recomputed after restoring the
best validation-loss checkpoint. Five fixed seeds were used: 13, 37, 71, 101,
and 137.

Model and hyperparameter selection was validation-only. Each candidate was run
on the validation partition across the prespecified seeds; the configuration
with the best mean validation objective was frozen and only then evaluated on
test. The selector rejects columns whose names indicate test outcomes. The
future MIL auxiliary weights $\{0.1,0.3,1.0\}$ and PU priors follow this same
policy.

The primary activity endpoint was macro test PR-AUC. We additionally reported
target-wise PR-AUC and prevalence, BEDROC ($\alpha=20$), EF1\% and EF5\%,
precision/recall at fixed experimental budgets, AUROC, Brier score, ten-bin
expected calibration error (ECE), and reliability curves. PR-AUC was emphasized for imbalanced screening
\citep{saito2015pr}, while BEDROC measures early recognition
\citep{truchon2007bedroc}. Paired 95\% intervals use 1,000 bootstrap replicates
with structure cluster as the resampling unit for compound endpoints and plant
as the unit for bag endpoints. Target-level secondary tests use
Benjamini--Hochberg correction.

The continuous sensitivity retained gray-zone observations and predicted median
pChEMBL. Mean absolute error (MAE) was the leading regression metric, supplemented
by root mean squared error and Spearman correlation. The natural-product
sensitivity joined the ChEMBL 37 molecule-dictionary flag generated from
COCONUT 2025-07. Subgroup results were declared descriptive unless a target had
at least 30 positives and 30 negatives.

Within each neural run, the test set was evaluated only after restoring the best
validation checkpoint. The source, dataset, run, metric, and prediction
artifacts are bound by SHA-256. Runs used Python 3.11.9, RDKit 2025.09.5,
scikit-learn 1.8.0, PyTorch 2.5.1 with CUDA 12.1, and Chemprop 2.2.1 on an
NVIDIA RTX 4060 Laptop GPU with 8 GB memory.

\subsection{Target-wise transfer and early recognition}

For the architecture-matched neural comparison, separate target MLPs exceeded
the shared activity-only MLP by 0.0077 macro PR-AUC. A hierarchical bootstrap
over seed and chemical component gave a 95\% interval of $-0.0141$ to
$-0.0021$ for shared minus separate. Target-wise differences were $-0.0271$
for AChE (95\% CI $-0.0463$ to $-0.0110$, BH-adjusted $p<0.001$), $+0.0045$
for DPP4 (95\% CI $-0.0015$ to 0.0110, adjusted $p=0.261$), and $-0.0005$
for PTGS2 (95\% CI $-0.0063$ to 0.0050, adjusted $p=0.900$). Negative transfer
was therefore localized to AChE rather than uniform across targets
(Figure~\ref{fig:targets}).

The shared MLP nevertheless increased macro BEDROC20 from 0.9714 to 0.9803 and
EF1\% from 1.2334 to 1.2527. The earliest-ranked candidates could thus improve
while global precision--recall and probability calibration deteriorated
(Figure~\ref{fig:enrichment}). These results make metric choice scientifically
material rather than cosmetic.

\subsection{Temporal and continuous-endpoint sensitivity}

The temporal experiment changed the conclusion about hard sharing. Extra Trees
remained highest at $0.9432\pm0.0008$, but the shared MLP was nearly tied at
$0.9425\pm0.0024$ and exceeded logistic regression
($0.9257\pm0.0000$) and separate MLPs ($0.9247\pm0.0029$). Shared-model
target PR-AUC was 0.9035 for AChE, 0.9721 for DPP4, and 0.9518 for PTGS2,
compared with 0.8993, 0.9792, and 0.8957 for separate models. The largest
temporal sharing gain was consequently on PTGS2.

Continuous prediction restored the negative-transfer pattern. Across 14,051
pChEMBL observations, separate MLPs achieved macro MAE
$0.8083\pm0.0066$, compared with $0.8181\pm0.0049$ for the shared model.
Per-target mean MAE was 0.7990 versus 0.8153 for AChE, 0.7306 versus 0.7326
for DPP4, and 0.8954 versus 0.9064 for PTGS2. Figure~\ref{fig:sensitivity}
summarizes the two sensitivity analyses. Taken together, the transfer direction
depended on both data split and endpoint definition.

\subsection{Natural-product subgroup and external-validity boundary}

The ChEMBL 37 molecule dictionary mapped 14,458 extracted molecules to a
COCONUT 2025-07 natural-product flag; 657 were flagged overall and 402 occurred
in the primary cohort. Only 79 flagged molecules reached the primary test set.
The target subsets comprised 55 AChE observations (11 active, 44 inactive),
seven DPP4 observations (five active, two inactive), and 19 PTGS2 observations
(nine active, ten inactive). None met the prespecified minimum of 30 positives
and 30 negatives, so all subgroup scores are descriptive and no natural-product
superiority claim is made.

We also assessed MoleculeACE \citep{vanTilborg2022moleculeace} as a possible
external benchmark. Its 30 ChEMBL activity-cliff datasets do not include
CHEMBL220, CHEMBL230, or CHEMBL284 and originate from the same database family.
It was therefore not relabelled as independent external validation.

We then audited the October 2026 article-curated BindingDB snapshot
\citep{liu2025bindingdb}. Eligibility required an exact human target accession,
single-chain target, exact Ki or IC50, a DOI or PMID absent from the ChEMBL 37
extract, no exact molecule overlap, and maximum Morgan Tanimoto below 0.70 to
the entire ChEMBL modelling cohort. After these exclusions, 233 labelled pairs
remained: DPP4 had 35 active and 23 inactive pairs, AChE had 32 and 87, and
PTGS2 had 17 and 39. Only AChE met the prespecified minimum of 30 observations
per class. We therefore computed no three-target external performance estimate
and make no independent-external-validation claim. The temporal holdout measures
chronological transport only.

\subsection{Implementation and confirmatory readiness}

All unit tests passed, including masked-loss, nnPU-risk, attention-MIL,
metric, temporal split, validation-only selection, and connected-component leakage invariants. A CUDA smoke run exercised
the activity-only, naive-broadcast, MIL, and shuffled-MIL paths end to end on
deterministic synthetic bags. Its manifest is explicitly marked
\texttt{scientific\_results=false}; therefore synthetic performance is not
reported. Botanical cold starts, PU-prior and auxiliary-weight sensitivity,
and attention stability require real bags and remain locked confirmatory
analyses. Detailed cohort audits, seed-level metrics, reliability bins, subgroup
results, and artifact hashes are provided in Additional file~1 and accompanying
machine-readable CSV files.

\section{Discussion}

This benchmark produced three practically important findings. First, the
strongest chemical-OOD ranking model was Extra Trees on fixed Morgan
fingerprints, not either neural architecture. This is a useful warning against
treating representation complexity as a substitute for stringent splitting and
strong classical controls. The D-MPNN result does not establish that molecular
graphs are generally inferior; it shows that the tested official implementation
and training budget did not overcome a well-tuned fixed representation on these
three endpoints.

Second, performance was multidimensional. D-MPNN had the weakest macro PR-AUC
but the best Brier score and ECE, while the shared MLP had competitive early
enrichment despite worse global ranking and calibration. Screening programs
that test only a small top-ranked fraction may reasonably prioritize BEDROC or
EF, whereas calibrated probabilities matter for risk estimates and decision
thresholds. Reporting only AUROC or a single aggregate score would have hidden
these differences.

Third, multi-target transfer was not an invariant property of the targets.
Hard sharing hurt chemical-OOD binary ranking and continuous potency prediction,
yet helped under temporal classification. The primary deficit was concentrated
in AChE; the temporal benefit was largest for PTGS2. A plausible interpretation
is that shared features regularize chronological extrapolation within recurring
chemical series but are less useful when all close fingerprint neighbours and
scaffolds are removed. This is an inference from the observed split contrast,
not a mechanistic conclusion, and should be tested across more targets.

The future VietHerb result must be interpreted as incremental predictive value,
not validation of traditional medicine. If MIL improves over activity-only
learning but not over shuffled labels, the gain is consistent with generic
regularization rather than semantic ethnopharmacological signal. If naive
broadcasting performs similarly to MIL, label granularity or leakage requires
further investigation. If all ethnomedical variants underperform, negative
transfer is an informative result and should not be hidden by selecting
favorable targets. The present finding that even measured-activity sharing can
reverse sign across regimes makes those controls essential.

Docking or short molecular dynamics may provide a later structure-based
plausibility assessment, but neither constitutes biological validation.
Docking scores have well-known limitations for affinity ranking
\citep{warren2006docking}. Prospective biochemical assays with positive,
negative, interference, and purity controls are required to claim target
inhibition.

This study has several limitations. Three targets cannot establish broad
cross-target generality, and ChEMBL measurements retain inter-laboratory and
assay heterogeneity after curation. The temporal holdout is not independent
external validation; a source-separated BindingDB audit was underpowered for
two of three targets, and the natural-product test subset was also underpowered. The
D-MPNN used one official version and budget rather than an exhaustive graph-model
search. Bootstrap intervals quantify sampling variation under the chosen
component structure but do not correct dataset-level selection bias. Finally,
the central plant-bag experiment is not yet empirical: incomplete plant chemical
inventories, uncertain traditional-use normalization, PU class-prior
uncertainty, and non-causal attention remain open risks. Written data permission
and domain review are scientific release gates, not administrative details.

\section{Conclusion}

We provide a version-pinned ChEMBL 37 benchmark in which 10,686 molecules passed
identity, scaffold, and fingerprint-neighbour leakage audits. Extra Trees was
the strongest chemical-OOD ranker, D-MPNN was the best-calibrated model, and the
direction of multi-target transfer changed between chemical-OOD, temporal, and
continuous endpoints. These findings argue for split-aware, target-wise, and
multi-metric evaluation with classical baselines. We also provide a tested
plant-bag MIL implementation and locked analysis plan, but the claim that
ethnopharmacological supervision improves molecular prioritization remains
neither supported nor refuted pending redistributable VietHerb data and
expert-audited mappings.
